voxy.speechtext¶

Display text vs speech text, and spotting words a model may misread.

A text a person reads and the text a model should be given are not always the same. Measured case (French, a Qwen3-TTS clone): a verse line starting with a capitalised verb, “Est parti” after a line “Mon frere”, was read as the noun /est/ (“l’Est”) in 4 of 4 takes; lowercased (“est parti”) it was read correctly in 4 of 4. The capital, not the line break, is the trigger: line-initial “Est” tokenises like the noun in “l’Est”. ASR checks cannot catch this (Whisper hears “est” either way), so the fix is upstream:

  1. prepare_speech_text() turns display text into speech text: it lowercases a verse-initial capital that does not start a sentence (keeping deliberate capitals: words also capitalised mid-line, or named in keep), then applies a per-language respelling table and proper-name aliases, and returns every change it made.

  2. flag_risks() lists the words of a text a model may misread (heteronyms you supply, e.g. from Lexique; more detectors plug in through extra), saying which ones sit where the capital trigger bites. pos_detector() is one such detector: it tags the text and, for heteronyms whose part of speech picks the reading (French “est”: verb /ɛ/, noun /ɛst/), names the expected reading.

Engines differ in what they accept: for several local models text is the only lever (they take no phonemes or SSML), so this is a text-level step; it plugs into LocalCloneSpeechModel(text_prep=...). Verifying the audio itself (phoneme recognition of the flagged words) is voxy.pronounce.

>>> text = "Mon frere\nEst parti\nLoin d'ici."
>>> prepare_speech_text(text).speech
"Mon frere\nest parti\nloin d'ici."
>>> prepare_speech_text(text, keep={"Est", "Loin"}).speech == text
True

voxy.speechtext.flag_risks(text, *, heteronyms=(), extra=())[source]¶

Words of text a model may misread, in text order.

Parameters:

  • text (str) – The text that will be spoken (run it through prepare_speech_text() first to see what is left).

  • heteronyms (Mapping[str, Iterable[str]] | Collection[str]) – Words with more than one reading, as a collection or as {word: readings} (e.g. from Lexique). Matched case-insensitively.

  • extra (Iterable[Callable[[str], Iterable[Risk]]]) – More detectors, each text -> risks (a part-of-speech rule, an LLM proposer). Their risks are merged in by line.

Return type:

list[Risk]

A heteronym that is capitalised at a line start where no sentence begins is flagged with line_initial_capital=True: that is where the capital trigger bites.

voxy.speechtext.pos_detector(rules={'est': {'ADJ': 'noun', 'AUX': 'verb', 'NOUN': 'noun', 'PROPN': 'noun', 'VERB': 'verb'}}, *, tagger=None)[source]¶

A flag_risks() extra detector: heteronyms whose part of speech picks the reading.

Run it on the speech text (prepare_speech_text()): a stray verse-initial capital fools the tagger the same way it fools the speech model.

Parameters:

  • rules (Mapping[str, Mapping[str, str]]) – {word: {UPOS tag: reading name}}, words matched case-insensitively (default FRENCH_POS_HETERONYMS).

  • tagger (Callable[[str], Iterable[tuple[int, str, str]]] | None) – text -> (offset, token, UPOS); default spacy_tagger() (French), loaded on first use.

Return type:

Callable[[str], list[Risk]]

>>> fake = lambda text: [(0, "Il", "PRON"), (3, "est", "AUX"), (7, "parti", "VERB")]
>>> [(r.word, r.expected) for r in pos_detector(tagger=fake)("Il est parti")]
[('est', 'verb')]

Display text in, speech text out, with a record of every change.

Parameters:

  • text (str) – The text as a reader sees it (lines may be verse lines).

  • lowercase_verse_initial (bool) – Lowercase a line-initial capital that does not start a sentence (the first line of a paragraph, or a line after one ending in . ! ? …, keeps its capital).

  • keep (Collection[str]) – Words whose capital is deliberate and stays (compared exactly). Words also capitalised elsewhere in a line are kept automatically.

  • respellings (Mapping[str, str] | None) – Word -> same-sound spelling, whole words, case-insensitive (e.g. a language’s ambiguous forms written unambiguously).

  • aliases (Mapping[str, str] | None) – Proper name -> pronounceable spelling, whole words, case-insensitive.

  • transforms (Iterable[Callable[[str], str]]) – Extra text -> text steps applied last, over the whole text.
