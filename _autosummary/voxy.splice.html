voxy.splice¶

Correct words in a recording, in the speaker’s voice: find, render, splice, check.

>>> from voxy.splice import replace_words
>>> fixed = replace_words("narration.wav", "Anna Smyth", "Anna Smith",
...                       voice="ada")
>>> fixed.passed, fixed.save("narration-fixed.wav")

Replace the whole coarticulated phrase, not one word: a surname spliced alone was heard with the wrong first consonant, because the first name before it anticipates the old surname’s lips; replacing first name and surname worked (voxy#38).

Steps, each a seam (keyword argument of replace_words):

  • words: the recording’s timed ASR words (default: transcriber(audio), voxy.refprep.transcribe); old is found among them (find_phrase).

  • render(text) -> (samples, sample_rate): the new phrase in the voice (default tts_render(voice): voxy.text_to_speech; a speech-to-speech conversion of a spoken correction, or a local clone, plug in the same way).

  • The cut lands at the quietest moment near each end of the phrase (cut_points): in the gap when there is one, and a little past ASR’s word times when there is none, since those are often early (a final /z/ left behind was heard as “Smith’s”). The render is trimmed, resampled, loudness-matched to what it replaces (level: by default the RMS of the loud frames on both sides, speech_level, so pauses do not count), and crossfaded. With keep_pauses (default), the pauses the cut removed are put back around the render from the cut’s own edges, so they keep their length and their background.

  • background(samples, sample_rate, length): what is mixed under the render so the recording’s floor does not drop out while the new words play (default quiet_background: the recording’s quietest frames, or nothing when even those are not quiet, as in wall-to-wall audio; None for no background).

  • verify: checks of the result (default heard_check: the new words are transcribed where they were put, and the old ones are gone; voice_check compares the new phrase’s voice print with the rest of the recording’s, voxy.speakers).

>>> import numpy as np
>>> sr = 8000
>>> tone = lambda s, amp: amp * np.sin(np.arange(int(s * sr)) / 3).astype(np.float32)
>>> gap = np.zeros(sr // 2)
>>> speech = np.concatenate([tone(1, .3), gap, tone(1, .3), gap, tone(.5, .3)])
>>> words = [{"text": "Hello", "start": 0.0, "end": 1.0},
...          {"text": "Smyth", "start": 1.5, "end": 2.5},
...          {"text": "there.", "start": 3.0, "end": 3.5}]
>>> fixed = replace_words(speech, "smyth", "Smith", words=words, sample_rate=sr,
...                       render=lambda text: (tone(0.8, 0.9), sr), verify=())
>>> fixed.replaced, round(fixed.start, 3), round(fixed.end, 3), fixed.passed
((1.375, 2.625), 1.48, 2.28, None)

voxy.splice.quiet_background(samples, sample_rate, length, *, share=0.05, drop_db=20.0, spread_db=6.0)[source]¶

length samples of the recording’s background, or None when it has none quiet enough to trust.

The background is made of the quietest share of its 10 ms frames, in time order, overlap-added (_overlap_add) and cycled to length. It is used only when every one of those frames is at least drop_db below speech_level and they are steady (within spread_db of each other): otherwise they would carry speech (a recording with no pause) or music (wall-to-wall audio), and nothing is better than that under the new words. O(len(samples)).

>>> rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
>>> x = np.concatenate([np.full(4000, 0.5), 0.005 * rng.standard_normal(2400)])
>>> bg = quiet_background(x, 8000, 5000)
>>> len(bg), round(float(np.sqrt(np.mean(bg**2))), 4)  # the floor's quietest frames
(5000, 0.0044)
>>> print(quiet_background(np.full(8000, 0.5), 8000, 5000))  # wall-to-wall
None
Return type:

ndarray | None

voxy.splice.replace_words(audio, old, new, *, voice=None, render=None, words=None, transcriber=None, sample_rate=None, occurrence=0, crossfade_ms=15, max_margin_ms=250, snap_ms=80, level=<function speech_level>, keep_pauses=True, background=<function quiet_background>, verify=None)[source]¶

Replace the spoken phrase old with new, rendered in voice.

Parameters:

  • audio – A WAV path or bytes (channels averaged), or raw mono samples (with sample_rate=; integer PCM is scaled to [-1, 1)).

  • old (str) – The phrase as spoken (several words: see the module docstring).

  • new (str) – What it should say.

  • voice – The speaker’s voice, for the default render (a library name, a backend voice, a profile). voice or render is required.

  • render (Callable[[str], tuple[ndarray, int]] | None) – text -> (samples, sample_rate); default tts_render(voice). A render that is silent raises ValueError.

  • words (Iterable[Mapping] | None) – Timed ASR words of audio; default transcriber(audio).

  • transcriber (Callable | None) – wav_path -> segments (default voxy.refprep.transcribe), for words and the default check.

  • occurrence (int) – Which occurrence of old (0: the first).

  • crossfade_ms (float) – Length of each crossfade.

  • max_margin_ms (float) – The furthest a cut may sit from the phrase, in a gap.

  • snap_ms (float) – How far past an ASR boundary a cut may look for quiet.

  • level (Callable[[ndarray, int], float]) – (samples, sample_rate) -> level the render is matched on; default speech_level (e.g. partial(speech_level, margin_db=10)).

  • keep_pauses (bool) – Put back, around the render, the pauses at the edges of the cut (taken from the cut itself, background included), so the pauses keep their length; False for the tighter splice.

  • background (Callable[[ndarray, int, int], ndarray | None] | None) – (samples, sample_rate, length) -> samples or None: what to mix under the render (None from it: nothing); default quiet_background, which gives nothing for audio with no quiet frames (wall-to-wall speech or music); None for no background.

  • verify (Callable[[ndarray, int, Replacement], dict] | Sequence[Callable[[ndarray, int, Replacement], dict]] | None) – A check or checks ((samples, sample_rate, replacement) -> dict with a passed key); default heard_check; () for none.

Return type:

Replacement

voxy.splice.tts_render(voice=None, *, backend=None, voices=None, use_library=True, model=None, **tts_kwargs)[source]¶

A render speaking the text with voxy.text_to_speech in voice.

The voice is resolved once, here (voxy.facade.resolve_voice, with voices, use_library and model’s backend), and every render synthesizes with what it resolved to. Backends whose default output is compressed get a WAV/PCM format (DFLT_RENDER_FORMATS) unless output_format= is given; other tts_kwargs go to text_to_speech.

Return type:

Callable[[str], tuple[ndarray, int]]

voxy.splice.voice_check(*, embed=None, min_similarity=None, window=None)[source]¶

A check: does the new phrase sound like the rest of the recording’s speaker?

Cosine similarity of the voice print of the new phrase and of the rest (voxy.speakers.voice_print, ECAPA in its own environment unless embed is given). min_similarity defaults to speakers.DFLT_MATCH_SIMILARITY (below it, a window counts as someone else); window (s) defaults to the speakers’ window, shortened to the phrase. Fails, with a reason, when the new phrase is the whole recording.

Callable[[ndarray, int, Replacement], dict]
