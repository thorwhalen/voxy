voxy.workers.soulx_adapter

voxy.workers.soulx_adapter¶

SoulX-Singer (Soul AI Lab; code and weights Apache-2.0): zero-shot singing voice synthesis (notes and lyrics to a sung vocal) and singing voice conversion.

Runs in the soulx environment, with no voxy imports. SoulX-Singer is not pip-installable, so load puts its checkout (repo_dir, fetched by voxy at a pinned commit) on sys.path. Weights come from the Hugging Face Hub at pinned revisions (into HF_HOME), unless weights_dir / preprocess_dir point at local copies.

Operations:

  • sing(state, *, meta, prompt_audio, prompt_meta, out_path, ...): SVS. meta and prompt_meta are SoulX metadata (lists of segments); prompt_audio is a sung clip whose notes and words prompt_meta describes. Empty phoneme fields are filled here (English, with g2p_en).

  • convert(state, *, audio, prompt_audio, out_path, ...): SVC, audio to audio; F0 is extracted with RMVPE.

  • f0(state, *, audio, out_path=None): the RMVPE F0 of a clip (50 frames a second).

  • unload(state, *, component): free one model (‘svs’, ‘svc’ or ‘rmvpe’).

The SVS and SVC checkpoints are 2.8 GB each; each loads on first use (preload loads them at start). On Apple Silicon the models run on MPS except the vocoder, which runs on the CPU: its ISTFT head multiplies complex tensors, which torch 2.2’s MPS backend rejects (an internal assert in BinaryKernel.mm). Output is written as 16-bit WAV, scaled down if it peaks above peak.

Usage disclaimer (upstream README): do not impersonate anyone without authorisation or make deceptive audio; respect intellectual property, privacy and consent.

voxy.workers.soulx_adapter.sing(state, *, meta, prompt_audio, prompt_meta, out_path, control='score', auto_shift=False, pitch_shift=0, n_steps=None, cfg=None, seed=None, peak=0.95, fade_s=0.02)[source]¶

SVS: sing meta in the voice of prompt_audio, into out_path.

control='score' follows the notes; 'melody' follows each segment’s f0. pitch_shift transposes by that many semitones; auto_shift (upstream’s median rule, recomputed for each segment) is best left off for a score of several segments. Each segment is placed at its time[0], with fade_s fades where segments meet.
