voxy.align

voxy.align¶

Where each word of a known text is in a recording, to the character: forced alignment.

ASR gives word times for what it heard; when the text is known (a script, lyrics, the words around a spot to edit), forced alignment places exactly those words, with each character’s span. That is what a splice needs at a word’s onset, and what a check needs when ASR heard a different word than the one spoken.

>>> from voxy.align import force_align
>>> spans = force_align("line.wav", "Mon frère est parti")
>>> spans[2]["word"], spans[2]["start"], spans[2]["chars"][0]

The default aligner is torchaudio’s MMS_FA (multilingual, Latin letters; accents are dropped for matching) in its own environment, built on first use (or python -m voxy setup align). Its weights are non-commercial (see workers/align_adapter.py). Align short stretches (a line, a word and its neighbours, under about 30 s): one pass over a long recording drifts, sung audio most.

run is the seam: (audio_path, words, *, start, end) -> [span or None, ...].

>>> fake = lambda audio, words, **crop: [{"start": i, "end": i + 0.5, "score": 0.9, "chars": []}
...                                      for i, _ in enumerate(words)]
>>> [s["word"] for s in force_align(b"RIFF....", "Bonjour  le monde", run=fake)]
['Bonjour', 'le', 'monde']

voxy.align.force_align(audio, text, *, start=None, end=None, run=None)[source]¶

Each word of text, in order, with its start, end (s), score (mean character confidence) and chars (char, start, end, score).

Parameters:

  • audio (str | PathLike | bytes) – A WAV (or any format soundfile reads) path, or WAV bytes.

  • text (str | Sequence[str]) – The words spoken, as a string (split on whitespace) or a list.

  • start (float | None) – The stretch of audio (s) the words are in (default: all of it); times stay on the recording’s clock.

  • end (float | None) – The stretch of audio (s) the words are in (default: all of it); times stay on the recording’s clock.

  • run (Callable[..., Sequence[dict | None]] | None) – (audio_path, words, *, start, end) -> [span or None, ...]; default worker_run().

Return type:

list[dict]

A word with no letter the aligner knows (a number, a symbol) is placed as one “any sound” token. Low scores (under about 0.5) mean the text is not what is said there. Audio over 30 s is refused by the default aligner: align in stretches. A run that returns None for a word gives start/end None.

voxy.align.which_said(audio, candidates, *, start=None, end=None, run=None, decisive_margin=1.0)[source]¶

The candidates (texts) ranked by how well the audio supports each, best first: [{"text", "score", "margin", "decisive"}]: score the aligner’s log-likelihood (minus its CTC loss; -inf for a text too long for the crop), margin the lead over the next candidate, decisive whether that lead is at least decisive_margin.

A forced choice, so it cannot hallucinate as transcribing a short crop can: use it to check an edit (“witch” or the old word?) on a crop around it (start/end, s). Cut the crop at silences and include the neighbouring words: a crop that ends inside a word, or holds one word alone, favours shorter texts by small margins, which decisive flags. Candidates are spelled in letters (numbers spelled out).

>>> fake = lambda audio, texts, start=None, end=None: [-5.0, -2.0]
>>> [(c["text"], c["margin"], c["decisive"])
...  for c in which_said(b"RIFF", ["bitch", "witch"], run=fake)]
[('witch', 3.0, True), ('bitch', None, None)]
