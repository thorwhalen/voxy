voxy.score

voxy.score¶

Scores for singing: the notes of a melody (pitch, timing, word) from MIDI, MusicXML or plain code, with lyrics attached.

A Note is one sung note in absolute seconds: its MIDI pitch, the word sung on it, and whether the word starts on it (starts_word) or continues from the note before (a melisma, or the later syllables of a word). Rests are the gaps between notes. A singing backend turns a list of notes into its own input format (voxy.soulx.soulx_metadata for SoulX-Singer).

>>> from voxy.score import read_score
>>> notes = read_score("melody.mid", "Light a can-dle in the win-dow")
>>> notes = read_score("song.musicxml")         # the score's own lyrics

Lyrics are a line of whitespace-separated tokens, one per sung note: can-dle spreads one word over two notes, a bare - (or _) holds the previous word one more note.

>>> notes = notes_from_pitches([(64, 1), (67, 1), (60, 2)], bpm=120)
>>> [(n.start, n.dur, n.pitch) for n in notes]
[(0.0, 0.5, 64), (0.5, 0.5, 67), (1.0, 1.0, 60)]
>>> [(n.word, n.starts_word) for n in attach_lyrics(notes, "hel-lo you")]
[('hello', True), ('hello', False), ('you', True)]

MIDI is read with mido (every tempo change honoured, lyric meta-events kept), MusicXML (and anything else music21 parses) with music21: both are in the voxy[singing] extra and imported only when a score of that kind is read.

voxy.score.notes_from_midi(path, *, track=None, channel=None, max_overlap_s=0.1, with_lyrics=True)[source]¶

The melody of a MIDI file: the notes of one track and channel, with its lyric events joined into words.

The line is the one track and channel that has notes, drums (channel 10) aside; with several, pass track= (its index in the file) and, for a file that mixes channels in one track, channel= (1 to 16); the error lists the candidates. Times follow the file’s tempo map. A note overlapping the next by up to max_overlap_s (legato) is cut at the next onset; more, or two notes starting together, is polyphony, and an error.

Words come from lyrics meta-events only (never text events, so a label is never sung), on the melody’s track, else on the one other track that has them (type-1 files often keep them in track 0). Each is a syllable on the note sounding at its tick (from a little before its onset to its end); one that falls on no note, or a second one on a note, is an error. A trailing - (can-) or a leading one (-dle) joins syllables into one word; a note without a syllable continues the word before it (a melisma), unless a rest separates them. with_lyrics=False reads no lyrics (words come from elsewhere). Karaoke files (.kar, lyrics in text events) are not supported yet: full score reading is planned in audiate (https://github.com/thorwhalen/audiate/issues/2).

Return type:

list[Note]

voxy.score.notes_from_musicxml(score, *, part=0)[source]¶

The notes of one part of a MusicXML file (or any score music21 reads, or a music21 stream), with its lyrics as words.

Only the part’s first voice is read (timed by the part’s own tempo marks), and only its first verse of lyrics; grace notes and zero-length notes are skipped; a chord sings its highest note. Tied notes become one. Syllables are joined into words with MusicXML’s syllabic (begin, middle, end), so can-dle on two notes is candle on both, the second continuing the word. A note without a lyric continues the word before it (a melisma under a slur), unless a rest came between.

Return type:

list[Note]

voxy.score.read_score(score, lyrics=None, *, filler='la', track=None, channel=None, part=0)[source]¶

The notes of score, with words: from lyrics if given, else the score’s own.

Parameters:

  • score – A MIDI file (.mid, .midi), a MusicXML file (.musicxml, .xml, .mxl, or anything music21 parses), a music21 stream, or a sequence of Note.

  • lyrics (str | None) – A lyric line (one token per note, see lyric_tokens); replaces the score’s own words.

  • filler (str) – The word for sung notes that still have none and continue no word (a note after a rest, in a score with lyrics on only some notes); a score with no words at all, and no lyrics, is an error.

  • track (int | None) – The MIDI line to sing (see notes_from_midi).

  • channel (int | None) – The MIDI line to sing (see notes_from_midi).

  • part (int) – The MusicXML part to sing.

Return type:

list[Note]

>>> [n.word for n in read_score(notes_from_pitches([(60, 1), (62, 1)]), "do re")]
['do', 're']
